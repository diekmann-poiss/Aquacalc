Project Summary

AquaCalc - Irrigation Water Calculator

About

AquaCalc helps landscape professionals and gardeners calculate irrigation water requirements based on the FAO-56 method.

Methodology

  • FAO Irrigation & Drainage Paper 56 (Allen et al., 1998)
  • ASCE-EWRI standardized reference ET equation (2005)
  • WUCOLS landscape water-use classification (Costello et al., UC Cooperative Extension)

Calculation Logic

  • Crop water use: ETc = ET₀ × Kc × situation demand factor
  • Effective rain: Rainfall × 0.35 × situation rain factor
  • Net irrigation: ETc minus effective rain, never below zero
  • Conversion: 1 mm over 1 m² = 1 litre

Climate Data Sources

  • Austria: ZAMG (Zentralanstalt für Meteorologie und Geodynamik), Klimanormalperiode 1981-2010
  • Germany: DWD (Deutscher Wetterdienst), Klimanormalperiode 1981-2010
  • Switzerland: MeteoSwiss, Klimanormalperiode 1981-2010
  • FAO CLIMWAT: Global cross-check for reference evapotranspiration and rainfall data. Access FAO CLIMWAT database

Precise Climate Data - Open-Meteo

Note: City climate values in this application are planning estimates following typical regional patterns for each city. They were not downloaded or checked station-by-station against the source institutions. The info panel lists the official reference sources, but the numbers themselves are estimates based on regional patterns.

For precise, location-specific climate data including ET₀ and rainfall, we recommend connecting to online climate services like Open-Meteo.

Open-Meteo provides free weather and climate data for any location worldwide, including all European cities and German cities for each Bundesland, using the FAO-56 Penman-Monteith reference evapotranspiration method.

Kc Value Sources

  • Primary: FAO Irrigation & Drainage Paper 56 (Allen et al., 1998) - Tab. 12 (various crops), Tab. 17 (trees)
  • Doorenbos, J.; Pruitt, W. O. (1977). 'Guidelines for predicting crop water requirements' - Climbing plants (Ivy, Grapevine)
  • Doorenbos, J.; Kassam, A. H. (1979). 'Yield response to water' - Trees (young/mature)
  • Pereira, L. S. et al. (2015). 'Crop Evapotranspiration: Principles and Design Applications' - Updated Kc values for grapevines and conifers

Data Notes

Kc values, situation factors, and flow rates are reasonable planning defaults, not measured data.

This implementation includes 200+ worldwide cities across all continents, all 16 German Bundeslaender, and 15+ plant types from the provided Excel data file. The original app description mentions ~70 cities and ~125 plant species, which may be in a more complete dataset.
